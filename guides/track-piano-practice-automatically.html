How to track piano practice automatically

Last updated 13 November 2026

Short answer: if you have a digital piano with USB or Bluetooth MIDI, an app that listens to the piano's MIDI can log your practice by itself. Simple ones count playing time and streaks; fuller ones also show what you played. Manual practice journals need a timer or an entry each time. A recorder box like Jamcorder logs automatically but costs about $185.

Why automatic tracking helps

  • Logging by hand takes effort. One adult learner's public log took "about 2–3 minutes per log" each day (benchanviolin.substack.com, October 2025). Missed entries are usually where a log gets abandoned.
  • Seeing progress keeps adults going. "Learners don't go back to the app because they stopped improving. They go back because they stopped being able to see it" (pract.is, June 2026).
  • Short sessions count. Adults practise around work and family, so a tracker should count a 10-minute session the same way as an hour-long one.

How to set it up with a digital piano

  1. Check that your piano has USB to Host (a square USB-B port) or Bluetooth MIDI. Most digital pianos from Roland, Yamaha, Kawai and Casio made since about 2015 have at least USB.
  2. Connect it to your phone with a USB-B to USB-C cable, or pair Bluetooth MIDI inside the app. See How to connect a Roland FP-30X to an iPhone.
  3. Keep the phone on the music stand. With Plink, practice is logged from the first note, with nothing to start or stop.

Someone else plays your piano?

An automatic tracker logs what's played while it's connected, including a child or a guest. With Plink, the logging stays on your phone, and you can pause recording for an hour or until tomorrow, or leave a session out afterwards ("Not you? Leave this out"). A left-out session doesn't count towards your streak, goal or stats.

Acoustic piano?

Automatic tracking works by reading MIDI, which acoustic pianos don't send. Your options are a practice timer or manual journal (Plink has a start/stop timer and lets you add past practice), a lesson app that listens through the microphone, or a silent or hybrid piano with MIDI out.
